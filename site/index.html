Two breaks, because eyes and legs want different things

Eye strain comes from hours of fixed focus. Stiffness comes from hours of not moving. One timer can't fix both, so MacBreak runs two.

👁️

Rest your eyes

every 30 min · 30 seconds

The 20-20-20 rule, enforced. Ten different prompts — look 20 metres away, roll your eyes slowly, palm them in the dark — so you keep reading instead of tuning it out. Want the rule exactly? Set 20 minutes and 20 seconds in Preferences.

🚶

Time for a walk

every 2 hours · 10 minutes

Long enough to actually leave the chair. Take a lap, refill your water the long way, go look at the sky. Finishing one resets the eye clock too.

Why it works when notifications don't

A banner you swat away without reading isn't a break. MacBreak covers every display and holds it.

  • No way to end it early. There's no Done button to reach for — the dial drains for the whole rest. The rest is the point, not the dismissal.
  • Nothing to click when it's over. At 00:00 the overlay fades away by itself and your clocks restart, even if you're still out on the walk.
  • Every display, every Space, above full-screen apps. It re-takes focus if another app grabs it, and swallows keystrokes so reflex typing goes nowhere.
  • Snooze is always one click away. A nudge with teeth, not a kiosk lock.

It knows when you've already rested

Lock your screen for lunch and MacBreak notices. Come back after five minutes or more and both clocks restart — no eye break thirty seconds after you sit down. Shorter locks leave the countdown alone.

And it stays out of your menu bar

A timer ticking all day is one more thing pulling at your attention. MacBreak shows one solid icon, tinted like macOS's own, and nothing else until a break is close.

Coming from Safe Eyes, Stretchly or another break timer?

Safe Eyes is a much-loved break reminder, but it only runs on Linux. Stretchly and BreakTimer run on a Mac through Electron. MacBreak is the native option: about 1,200 lines of Swift and AppKit, using around 10 MB of memory while it waits.

Install

Two ways in. Both take about a minute.

Homebrew
brew install --cask deepakk33/tap/macbreak
open -a MacBreak   # then tick "Start at login"
Or build from source
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/deepakk33/macbreak/main/scripts/bootstrap.sh | bash

Compiles on your machine, so there's no Gatekeeper prompt and nothing to trust that you can't read first. Needs xcode-select --install — not the full Xcode.

Then

Press Cmd-Space, type macbreak, and set your intervals. Seven of them: how often each break comes, how long each lasts, snooze length, how long a screen lock counts as rest, and when the menu bar is allowed to start counting.

Questions

Does it work with multiple monitors?

Yes. Every display is covered, each with its own countdown and buttons.

Is MacBreak free?

Yes. It is free and open source under the MIT licence, with no paid tier, no account and no ads.

Does it work on Intel Macs and older macOS?

Yes. Every build is universal — Apple Silicon and Intel — and runs on macOS 13 Ventura or later.

Is there a version of Safe Eyes for Mac?

Not officially: Safe Eyes is built for Linux. MacBreak covers the same ground natively on macOS — full-screen eye breaks, longer movement breaks, and breaks that can't be dismissed early. Read the comparison.

How do I follow the 20-20-20 rule on a Mac?

Install MacBreak, open Preferences, and set "Look away every" to 20 minutes and "Look away for" to 20 seconds. Every 20 minutes the screen asks you to look at something at least 20 feet away. More on the rule.

Does it phone home?

No. There is no network code in the repository — you can check. No account, no analytics, nothing leaves your machine.

Is it code-signed?

Not yet. The Homebrew cask clears the download quarantine for you. Building from source sidesteps the question entirely.

Can I use it for eyes only?

Set the walk interval very high in Preferences, and it effectively disappears.

What if I'm in a meeting?

Snooze, or Pause from the menu bar. Pause clears both clocks until you resume.

How do I get rid of it?

brew uninstall --cask macbreak, or make uninstall from the source checkout. Both stop it and remove the login agent.

Why not Electron?

A break reminder that idles at 200 MB is its own kind of problem. This is AppKit — about 1,200 lines of Swift, around 10 MB of memory, no dependencies, no runtime.
