Safe Eyes for Mac? Here's the native alternative

Short answer: Safe Eyes doesn't run on macOS — it's a Linux app. If you want the same thing on a Mac, breaks that take over the screen instead of a notification you swat away, MacBreak is a free, open-source, native equivalent.

Updated 9 October 2026 · 6 minute read

Does Safe Eyes work on macOS?

Safe Eyes is a well-loved break reminder for Linux, written in Python with GTK 4 and licensed under the GPL-3.0. It ships through Linux channels — distribution packages, the Ubuntu PPA, the Arch AUR, Flathub and PyPI — and its installation guide covers Linux distributions only. There is no official macOS version.

That leaves Mac users who liked Safe Eyes on a desktop machine, or who just heard it recommended, looking for something that behaves the same way on macOS.

What carries over from Safe Eyes

Most of what Safe Eyes does has a direct counterpart in MacBreak:

In Safe EyesIn MacBreak
Short breaksLook-away breaks — every 30 minutes for 30 seconds by default; set them to 20 and 20 for the 20-20-20 rule
Long breaksWalk breaks — every 2 hours for 10 minutes by default
Full-screen break windowAn overlay on every display, across all Spaces and above full-screen apps
Strict breakAlways on: there's no way to end a break early, only Snooze, which defers it
PostponeSnooze, 10 minutes by default
Break exercisesTen eye prompts and eight movement prompts, drawn at random
Smart pauseNo idle tracking, but locking your screen for 5 minutes or more counts as a break and restarts both clocks
PluginsNone. MacBreak is deliberately small: about 1,200 lines of Swift

One difference worth knowing: when a MacBreak break is over, the overlay closes on its own. There's nothing to click to get your screen back.

When another app is the better pick

If you switch between a Mac and Windows or Linux and want one tool everywhere, Stretchly or BreakTimer run on all three. If you want breaks to pause automatically when you step away from the keyboard, Stretchly and Safe Eyes watch for idle time and MacBreak doesn't. On Linux, Safe Eyes is still an excellent choice.

Why MacBreak on a Mac

  • Native. AppKit, no Electron, around 10 MB of memory while it waits. A break reminder shouldn't be the heaviest thing running.
  • Breaks you can't click away. No Done button during the rest. The countdown is the point.
  • Nothing to click when it's over. The overlay fades by itself at 00:00 and the clocks restart.
  • Every display, every Space. It sits above full-screen apps, takes focus back if another app grabs it, and swallows keystrokes.
  • It knows you've rested. A screen lock of five minutes or more counts as a break.
  • Quiet menu bar. A single solid icon until a break is close.
  • Free and private. MIT licensed, no account, no telemetry, no network code.

Moving your Safe Eyes settings over

Open MacBreak's Preferences (Cmd-Space, type macbreak). Copy your Safe Eyes short-break interval and length into Look away every and Look away for, and your long-break ones into Walk break every and Walk for. Note that MacBreak counts the long break in minutes. Tick Start at login and save.

Questions

Is there an official Safe Eyes for Mac?

No. Safe Eyes is developed for Linux, and its installation guide covers Linux distributions only. MacBreak is a separate, independent project that brings the same kind of full-screen breaks to macOS.

Is MacBreak affiliated with Safe Eyes?

No. MacBreak is an independent open-source project. Safe Eyes is mentioned here only to help Mac users find an equivalent.

Is MacBreak free like Safe Eyes?

Yes. It is free and open source under the MIT licence, with no paid tier, no account and no ads.

Does MacBreak run on Windows or Linux?

No, it is macOS only. On Linux, Safe Eyes is a great choice; for Windows, Stretchly and BreakTimer are cross-platform.

What's the best Stretchly alternative for Mac?

If you want a native app rather than an Electron one, MacBreak covers the same core — short eye breaks, longer movement breaks, breaks that can't be ended early — in around 10 MB of memory.
